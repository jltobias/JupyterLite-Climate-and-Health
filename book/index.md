# Climate & Health: a field guide for a changing world

Climate affects health through many connected pathways: heat exposure, disrupted ecosystems, food and water access, livelihoods, mobility and health-service continuity. This course helps public-health students and practitioners ask useful questions, test arithmetic and communicate the limits of evidence.

Start with the [browser guide](start.md), then choose a learning path. Each lesson contains a small executable analysis, an exercise, interpretation and references. The reading edition includes executed outputs; the [Python Lab](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-Climate-and-Health/lite/lab/index.html?path=notebooks/00_start_here.ipynb) lets you edit and run the same notebooks without installation.

## Three connected learning paths

| Your question | Suggested lessons | Put it into practice |
|---|---|---|
| What does the climate evidence say? | 00–03: provenance, NASA global anomalies, ensembles and daily heat | Explain a baseline and distinguish hot-day counts from a heatwave |
| How can climate disrupt care? | 04–10: cities, coasts, HIV/TB continuity, movement, reefs, food/water and compound hazards | Trace a service dependency and request the missing local evidence |
| Which responses deserve investigation? | 11–12: adaptation, equity and capstone | Compare assumptions, build a map story and explain uncertainty |

Use the [interactive explorer](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-Climate-and-Health/explore/) for 2D maps, a rotatable extruded 3D map, a conceptual 3D clinic/coast scene, a guided story and adaptation comparisons. The [satellite catalog gateway](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-Climate-and-Health/stac/) extends data discovery to Copernicus and other environmental sources. Catalog metadata is not a downloaded or validated hazard analysis.

## Our evidence contract

**Observed:** NASA GISTEMP v4 is a dated snapshot of global annual temperature anomalies relative to 1951–1980. Natural Earth provides coarse reference geography. An archived Healthsites/OpenStreetMap sample across seven city contexts represents mapped public facility features, with completeness and currency limitations.

**Synthetic:** local daily temperature series, clinic attributes, capacities, costs, movement flows and scenario results are original invented teaching data. They do not represent PEPFAR facility operations, observed impacts, forecasts or country risk rankings. The NASA global series is never used to claim local heat or disease exposure.

India, Mozambique, South Africa, Kenya, Zambia, Uganda and Nigeria provide geographic context. This selection is not a complete list of PEPFAR partner countries and does not assert actual funding of mapped facilities. [CDC describes its PEPFAR-supported work in India](https://www.cdc.gov/global-hiv-tb/php/where-we-work/india.html).
